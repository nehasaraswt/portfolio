Colour, material, finish as an argument

Design Workshop III · B.Des Textile Design · NID Ahmedabad · Visiting faculty · 20265 trend studies · 13 product briefs · 7 product categories
the concept and the craft, at the same time
16textile design students, in six days

I taught sixteen textile design students to treat colour, material and finish as an argument they can defend to a person, a product and a maker.

Colour, material and finish are easy to treat as decoration: a pleasing palette, a list of materials, a finish named in a caption. I wanted students to treat them as a position. Every choice should trace back to a tension in the world, a specific person and a specific product, and the sample should be able to make the case on its own.

The method adapts Futurecasting in 5 Steps, which I facilitated with design teams at Intuit, Walmart and [24]7.ai, and applies it to textile CMF.

The arc, from trend to sample
Step 1 · Signals
Curate, don't spot.Students map published signals on a wheel of opportunity before they name anything.
Step 2 · Trend
A tension, in their own words.Never a forecaster's label.
Step 3 · Person
A specific human.Someone with a life, not a demographic.
Step 4 · Brief
One key colour, named first.And one question the sample must answer.
Step 5 · Sample
Make it, then show it in silence.Test it against the question, and show it before anyone explains it.
The test I use everywhere
Cover the labels.If you still get it, the work speaks through the material. If you don't, it is only talking about it. In crit: thirty seconds with the sample, no brief, no explanation. Write down what you think the trend is. If the guess is close, the work is doing its job.

How I assessed

Students were graded on NID's five criteria. Behind them sits a six-part evidence rubric, so every grade has a stated reason. Neither concept without craft nor craft without concept can score high alone.

Trend and personaIs there a real tension, and a real person, underneath the CMF choices?15%
Brief and translationDoes the brief do the translation work, trend into colour, material and finish, for this specific product?20%
Material craftDoes the physical sample hold up as a piece of work?25%
Coherence of the wholeDo the trend, persona, brief, sample, board and narrative all argue the same thing?20%
Narrative voiceDoes the writing make you feel the trend before you understand it?12%
Rigour and feasibilityCould this brief go to a manufacturer? Is the process visible, not just the output?8%

Each student received four letter grades with a one-line basis for each, and a remark of 300 characters or fewer: one strength, one fix.

What the cohort showed me
Students can name a territory and a person.Five territories in their own language, and most briefs carried a specific persona.
The gap is the hand.Most briefs stayed in rendered images rather than handmade material samples.
Range crowds out decision.Many briefs ended on a shortlist where the brief asked for one answer.
The line I keep

A palette is a mood. A key colour is a decision.
