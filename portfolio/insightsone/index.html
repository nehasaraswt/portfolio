I was called in to design the dashboard, and ended up designing the platform behind it.

The platform predicted a customer's propensity to buy, to come back to the cart and finish the journey, to move along the funnel, and to react to an impression or a click-through.

There was no platform. The models were hand-coded and deployed every time a new requirement came in. When I saw the process, I asked if I could try to abstract what the data scientists did, and see if it could be visualised. The first piece we designed was finalising a segment. The rest followed from how easy it became to define a segment to target, much like a pixel on Facebook's platform.

Who used itMarketers at Fab.comApigeeOther retail customers
Three decisions
Decision 1
A dashboard is not the platform.The value was in what produced the numbers, so that is what we designed.
Decision 2
Two layers, kept apart.The data scientist's workbench on one side, and the customer-facing layer where dimensions are tuned on the other.
Decision 3
Observable and actionable.The platform shows what is happening, and lets people act on it in the same place.
