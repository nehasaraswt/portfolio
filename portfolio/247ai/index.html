I came for the insights platform and found it needed a system. A bot and a human agent, I realised, are the same entity, and one data taxonomy can serve both.

[24]7.ai served enterprises like Cisco, Hilton, Nike Football, Target and Optus across chat, voice, IVR and digital. The platform had been put together through acquisitions: 15 products, 50 different interfaces and 15 kinds of users, each product on its own data model.

Most people downloaded reports and worked in Excel, and the decisions that could not wait were made on gut feel. My first pitch was against making more dashboards: serve the data where people act on it. To do that, I first had to understand the system.

Three decisions
Agents are one category
A bot and a human are the same entity.They differ by what they can do, not by what they are. Both draw on the same intent data and the same responses, so a handoff loses nothing.
The handoff
A briefing, not a failure state.When AIVA passed a conversation to a person, the agent saw the intent, the history and a suggested first reply, and carried on instead of starting over.
One taxonomy
One set of numbers for everyone.The taxonomy related information across the platform into one system. It fixed the formulae and deductions, defined the KPIs, and said which user should care most about which one.
The system, earned
Workforce management, from the agent up

I started with the user every workforce tool had overlooked: the agent on the floor. The tools of the time were built for whoever bought them, not for the people who used them every shift.

Before any screen, I mapped four roles across four time horizons, and how each one's decisions fed the others. Agents got a portal built around subtraction: their schedule, a swap request and their adherence, nothing more. Team leads got a live situation board. Planners got forecasting they could trust at scale.

Project Heisenberg, the design system

Teams that had never worked together were expected to ship one platform, and it looked like six companies. I started Project Heisenberg to standardise form, fit and finish across 12 products.

It was crowd-sourced, not handed down. Five designers, each owning a product area, built the system they would use, and the org-wide design sprints I ran became how teams adopted it. Themeroller let 37 clients carry their own brand inside the system's rules.

What it taught me

You can't draw the system on day one. You earn it, one piece at a time, until enough of the map is in place that the whole becomes visible.
